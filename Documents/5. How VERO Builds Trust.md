# How VERO Builds Trust

VERO's entire value depends on one thing: the records it creates are genuinely trustworthy. This document explains how that works — what makes a VERO record hard to fake, what happens when something goes wrong, and how trust grows over time.

---

## The Foundation — Dual Signature

Every verified work record on VERO requires two signatures: one from the worker, one from the client.

This is not a technicality. It is the core mechanism that makes the system resistant to manipulation:

- A worker cannot claim to have done work the client did not confirm
- A client cannot deny that work was completed if they already signed
- Neither side can create a record alone
- If one side refuses to sign without a legitimate reason, there is a clear dispute path

The dual-signature requirement means that every record on VERO represents a real agreement between two real people about real work that happened.

---

## What Makes a Record Hard to Fake

**Verified identity** — both parties confirmed who they are before they could participate. Anonymous accounts cannot create records.

**Pre-agreed scope** — what was supposed to happen was written down and agreed to before the work started. There is a baseline to compare against.

**Escrow for paid work** — money was committed and held before the job began. Both sides had real financial stakes.

**Linked history** — each record is connected to all previous records. You cannot drop a suspicious gap in your history or suddenly have 50 completed jobs after being inactive for a year without it showing.

**Behavioral signals** — VERO monitors patterns that suggest gaming: sudden spikes in activity, records between accounts that seem connected, repeated cancellations, low-quality dispute patterns. These are flagged and reviewed.

**Permanent records** — once signed, nothing can be quietly edited. If something was later disputed or reversed, that is also recorded — the full history is visible, not just the highlights.

---

## Trust Signals (What Makes Up Your Standing)

Your trust standing on VERO is not a single number. It is made up of multiple independent signals:

- **Completion rate** — how many jobs you started and actually finished
- **Punctuality** — how often you delivered on time versus late
- **Repeat client rate** — how many clients have hired you more than once
- **Cancellation rate** — how often you backed out of agreed work
- **Dispute rate and outcomes** — how many disputes you have been involved in, and how they were resolved
- **Client endorsements** — signed statements from people you have worked for, weighted by their own track record
- **Verified specialisations** — marks for specific skills earned through completed work, not self-selected from a list
- **Consistency over time** — whether your track record is stable and reliable, or erratic

No single signal can make or break your standing. A low dispute rate, high completion rate, and several repeat clients together tell a much more reliable story than any one metric.

## What Happens When Things Go Wrong — The Dispute System

VERO does not assume every complaint is legitimate, and it does not assume every worker is at fault. The dispute system is designed to be fair to both sides.

**Tier 1 — Direct resolution**
The worker and client talk it out. Most disagreements are resolved here — a misunderstanding about scope, a delivery timing issue, a minor quality concern. VERO provides the space for this conversation. Both sides can upload evidence.

**Tier 2 — Mediation**
If direct resolution fails, a VERO mediator reviews the evidence: the original agreed scope, any communication during the job, the delivery, and both sides' accounts. The mediator proposes a resolution. Either side can accept or escalate.

**Tier 3 — Formal review**
A formal VERO review examines the complete record and makes a binding decision. This outcome is logged permanently. It affects the trust standing of whichever party acted in bad faith.

Every step of every dispute is logged. This prevents either side from claiming the other behaved badly without evidence.

---

## How VERO Protects Clients

- Workers are identity-verified before they can accept paid work
- Payment is held in escrow — clients never pay into a void
- If a worker fails to deliver and refuses to engage, the dispute system recovers the payment
- Fraud patterns are monitored continuously

---

## How VERO Protects Workers

- Clients must fund escrow before work begins — workers do not work unpaid
- Clients cannot withhold payment arbitrarily if work was completed as agreed
- Workers own their record permanently — no platform can delete or modify it
- Workers have a clear path if a client acts unfairly

---

## Anti-Gaming

VERO actively works to prevent reputation manipulation:

- Fake accounts: multiple accounts from the same person are detected and removed
- Collusive reviews: networks of people giving each other positive records without real work are identified through pattern analysis
- Trust farming: recording low-value or fake jobs just to inflate history is flagged
- Coordinated gaming: accounts that seem to be working together to manipulate the system are reviewed

The goal is a record system where the only way to build a good reputation is to do good work consistently over time.

---

## The ALVED Protocol

VERO's trust system is built on a shared protocol called **ALVED** — Authentic Ledger of Validated Evolution Data. This is the underlying architecture for how trust signals are collected, weighted, and stored.

ALVED is designed to be:
- Auditable — every trust signal has a traceable source
- Explainable — users can understand why their standing is what it is
- Anti-gaming — multiple independent signals make it hard to manipulate any single one
- Longitudinal — your trust grows (or erodes) over time based on real behavior, not one-off events

You do not need to understand the technical details of ALVED to use VERO. But if you are building on top of VERO or want to understand how the trust graph works, ALVED is the place to start.
