# How VERO Works — The User Experience

VERO works in five steps. Every step is designed to create a real, verified record that both the worker and the client have agreed to. The process is the same whether you are a designer delivering a logo or a plumber fixing a pipe.

---

## The Five Steps

### Step 1 — Verify once

Before anything else, every person on VERO — worker or business — confirms their identity. This is not optional. Anonymous accounts cannot create verified records.

For workers: phone number, name, and optionally a government-issued ID.
For businesses: business name, contact, and optionally a GST number.

This happens once. After that, your verified status stays with you.

**Why this matters:** A verified record is only as trustworthy as the people who signed it. If anyone can create an account without verification, the whole system fails.

---

### Step 2 — Agree on the work

Before any work starts, both the worker and the business agree in writing on what needs to be done. This is the scope — what will be delivered, when, and for how much.

For paid work, the money is held in escrow (a protected payment account) before the job starts. The worker knows the money is real. The business knows it is safe until the work is confirmed complete.

**Why this matters:** Disputes almost always start because two people had different expectations. Written agreement eliminates that. Escrow eliminates payment risk for both sides.

---

### Step 3 — Do the work

A plumber fixes the tap. A designer delivers the logo. A developer ships the feature. A chef cooks the meal.

Real work, real people. Nothing abstract. VERO does not get involved in how the work is done — only in verifying that it happened.

**Why this matters:** VERO is not a micromanagement platform. It trusts workers and businesses to do their jobs. It is only in the record-keeping that it plays a role.

---

### Step 4 — Both sides sign

When the work is done, both the worker and the client confirm it. Both signatures are required. Neither side can create a record alone.

If there is a dispute — the work was not what was agreed, or payment is in question — there is a clear path: direct conversation first, then a VERO mediator, then a formal review. Every step is logged.

**Why this matters:** The dual-signature requirement is the heart of what makes VERO records trustworthy. A worker cannot claim to have done work the client did not confirm. A client cannot withhold payment for work that was completed as agreed.

---

### Step 5 — The record is yours forever

When both sides sign, a permanent record is created. It is linked to all your previous records, forming a chain of verified work history. It cannot be edited, deleted, or taken away from you.

The record belongs to the worker — not to VERO, not to the client, not to any platform. If VERO shut down tomorrow, your work history would still exist and could be exported.

**Why this matters:** The most important thing VERO does is give workers ownership of their own professional reputation. Your work is yours. Your record is yours.

## What the Worker Experience Looks Like

1. Sign up, get verified (takes a few minutes)
2. Set up your profile: skills, career path, portfolio samples
3. Browse available jobs by category and city area
4. Apply to jobs that match your skills (one screen, no bidding)
5. Agree on scope with the business, fund escrow if paid
6. Do the work
7. Submit what you delivered, trigger the signature request
8. Business confirms and signs — you get paid, your record updates
9. Your public work profile shows the completed, signed job

---

## What the Business Experience Looks Like

1. Sign up, verify your business
2. Post a job: category, scope, budget, location
3. Review applicants — see their verified work record, signed history, trust standing
4. Choose who to hire, fund escrow
5. Worker completes the job
6. Confirm and sign — payment releases to the worker
7. Your dashboard shows all active and completed hires

---

## What Happens If Something Goes Wrong

VERO has a three-tier dispute system:

1. **Direct** — worker and client resolve it between themselves (most disputes end here)
2. **Mediated** — a VERO mediator reviews the evidence and proposes a resolution
3. **Reviewed** — a formal VERO review looks at the full record and makes a binding decision

Every step is logged. Neither side can erase or edit what happened. The goal is fairness for both sides — not always finding in favour of one or the other.

---

## What a Verified Work Record Looks Like

Your public work record on VERO shows:

- Every verified job you have completed, in order, with both signatures
- What was built, fixed, shipped, or delivered (linked to output if both sides agree)
- How your standing has grown over time
- Endorsements from clients you have worked for, weighted by their own track record
- Verified specialisations earned through real completed work
- Option to export your record and share it outside VERO
