# Investor Narrative

## Rule

This is an evidence-based narrative, not a place to manufacture a compelling story.

## One sentence

What is the company/product and why does it matter?

## Problem

Evidence that the problem exists and matters.

## Insight

The non-obvious insight.

## Product

What has been built and what users actually experience.

## Why now

Verified reasons the timing matters.

## Market

Market definition, methodology, source and date.

## Business model

Pricing, monetization and economics.

## Traction

Only verified metrics. Include measurement period and definition.

## Growth

Acquisition channels, retention, expansion and evidence.

## Competition

Alternatives, strengths, weaknesses and differentiation.

## Moat

What becomes stronger with scale, data, distribution, technology, brand, network effects or execution?

## Roadmap

Only include commitments supported by the product thesis.

## Risks

Material risks and mitigation.

## Capital

Amount requested, use of funds and expected milestones.

## Founder advantage

Only factual, relevant evidence.

## Change / investment decision

For a material investment, product, infrastructure or growth change:

### Why should we make this change?

### What impact will it have?

### What results should it yield?

### How will we do it?

### How much will it cost?

### Is the cost justified?

Always compare expected value with cost and use the lowest-cost approach that satisfies the requirement.

## Claim audit

Every material number or market claim must have a source.

## Investor quality gate

Reject any slide containing:
- fabricated metrics
- unsupported market claims
- fake customer quotes
- guaranteed outcomes
- unexplained projections
- claims contradicted by `RESEARCH.md`
