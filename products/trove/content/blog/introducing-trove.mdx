For most of my twenties, I did what every reasonable person does about their money: I ignored it, mostly. Then I bought a spreadsheet template, used it for two months, abandoned it. Then I tried Mint, then YNAB, then a custom Notion dashboard that lasted exactly one quarterly review.

The problem wasn't the tools. The problem was that none of them treated me — or you, probably — like an adult.

## What Trove is

Trove is a financial operating system. That's a deliberately specific phrase.

- An *operating system* sits underneath everything else. You don't think about it. It just works.
- A *financial* one means it sees every account, every transaction, every subscription — and helps you decide.

Trove connects your accounts, categorizes everything, surfaces the recurring spend you forgot, summarizes your month in plain language, and — when you ask — helps you make the next decision.

## What Trove is not

- It is not a bank. We can't move your money.
- It is not a budgeting app. Budgets are one feature among many.
- It is not a robo-advisor. We don't tell you what stocks to buy.
- It is not free of opinions. We think most subscriptions should be cancelled.

## How we built it

V1 ran on Vite + Supabase, served 200+ users, and proved the thesis. V2 — what you're using today — is a full rewrite on Next.js, with a design system that took longer to make than the original product. The UI is Financial Times × Apple. Cream, gold, serif. No gradients. No noise.

## What comes next

Through Q3, we're focused on three things:

1. Plaid integration for U.S. banks (read-only).
2. AI Insights V2 — a single weekly digest that replaces dashboards you forget to open.
3. iOS — the canonical Trove experience belongs on a phone.

Thank you for being here at the start.
